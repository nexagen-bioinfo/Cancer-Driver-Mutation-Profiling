# Contributor Code of Conduct

## 1. Our Pledge

As contributors, maintainers, and researchers in the **Scurator** project (High-Performance, GPU-Accelerated Somatic Driver Mutation Profiling Framework), we pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free, inclusive, and intellectually rigorous experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We are committed to fostering an open science environment aligned with European Open Science Cloud (EOSC) FAIR principles and Google Open Source Software (OSS) engineering practices.

---

## 2. Expected Behavior and Community Standards

We encourage behaviors that contribute to a healthy, collaborative, and academically rigorous research environment:

* **Demonstrating Empathy and Respect:** Treat every community member with professional courtesy, regardless of their level of bioinformatics expertise.
* **Constructive Scientific Critique:** Focus code reviews and technical feedback on algorithms, code quality, data structures, and biological validity rather than individuals.
* **Respecting Differing Scientific Perspectives:** Value diverse methodological approaches to cancer driver mutation identification, variant effect prediction, and hardware acceleration.
* **Commitment to Open Science and Reproducibility:** Share code, synthetic test fixtures, and technical methodology transparently to enable peer review and scientific verification.
* **Gracefully Accepting Criticism:** Welcome constructive feedback on pull requests and algorithmic implementations with scientific humility.

---

## 3. Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors are considered unacceptable within the Scurator community:

* **Harassment and Abusive Language:** Sexualized language or imagery, unwelcome sexual attention, or advances of any kind.
* **Personal or Political Attacks:** Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or ad hominem attacks.
* **Public or Private Harassment:** Repeated unwanted contact, intimidation, or stalking across GitHub issues, pull requests, or private channels.
* **Unauthorized Publication of Private Information:** Publishing others' private information, such as physical or electronic addresses, or Protected Health Information (PHI) without explicit permission.
* **Scientific Misconduct:** Deliberate falsification of benchmarking metrics, plagiarizing bioinformatics code, or introducing malicious code snippets into the framework.
* **Disruptive Behavior:** Sustained disruption of technical discussions, issue trackers, or community meetings.

---

## 4. Academic and Data Integrity Standards

Given Scurator's role in cancer genomics and medical informatics, contributors must adhere to strict ethical standards regarding data privacy and scientific integrity:

* **Genomic Data Privacy:** Do not submit VCF files, BAM alignments, or tabular datasets containing identifiable human patient information or Protected Health Information (PHI). All test fixtures must be synthetic or derived from open-access datasets (e.g., ClinVar, TCGA public tier).
* **Intellectual Property and Licensing:** Respect intellectual property rights. Ensure all code contributions are compatible with the European Union Public Licence v1.2 (EUPL-1.2) and properly cited where external algorithms or published papers are referenced.
* **Conflict of Interest Transparency:** Disclose any financial, commercial, or institutional conflicts of interest when submitting benchmarks comparing Scurator against third-party bioinformatics tools.

---

## 5. Responsibilities of Maintainers

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior. They will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any instances of unacceptable behavior.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code edits, issue reports, wiki pages, and pull requests that do not align with this Code of Conduct, or to temporarily or permanently ban any contributor for behaviors they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

---

## 6. Scope of Application

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces managed by Scurator, including:

* The official GitHub repository (`https://github.com/nexagen-bioinfo/Cancer-Driver-Mutation-Profiling.git`).
* GitHub Issues, Pull Request reviews, Discussions, and Wiki pages.
* Official project communication channels, mailing lists, and community forums.
* Public academic conferences, workshops, or scientific presentations where individuals represent the Scurator project.

---

## 7. Reporting Guidelines and Incident Handling

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be reported to the Scurator Conduct Committee by emailing:

**conduct@nexagen.org**

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and confidentially. The conduct committee is obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.

### Information to Include in a Report

To help us investigate the incident, please include:

1. Your contact information (email address or GitHub username).
2. Names or GitHub handles of any individuals involved.
3. A detailed description of the incident, including dates, times, and relevant URLs or screenshots.
4. Any additional context or previous interactions related to the incident.

---

## 8. Enforcement Process and Escalation Path

When an incident is reported, maintainers will follow these enforcement steps based on the severity of the violation:

### Step 1: Correction
* **Community Impact:** Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional in the community.
* **Consequence:** A private, written warning from maintainers, providing clarity regarding the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### Step 2: Warning
* **Community Impact:** A violation through a single incident or series of actions demonstrating unprofessional conduct.
* **Consequence:** A formal warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding space in community channels.

### Step 3: Temporary Ban
* **Community Impact:** A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior or personal attacks.
* **Consequence:** A temporary ban from any form of interaction or public communication with the project community for a specified period (e.g., 30 days). No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period.

### Step 4: Permanent Ban
* **Community Impact:** Demonstrating a pattern of disruptive behavior, sustained harassment, publication of private genomic/PHI data, or scientific misconduct.
* **Consequence:** A permanent ban from any form of public interaction within the project community, including all GitHub repositories and project communication channels.
